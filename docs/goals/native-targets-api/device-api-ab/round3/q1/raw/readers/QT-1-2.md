## Ranking
1. VR — Each block names the platforms directly (`platform.ios || platform.android`, `platform.macos`), so you can read off exactly what the website and the iPhone get without guessing what "supported" means. The catch is that it is hand-written and may be wrong: it gives the Mac app the "works in the app" fallbacks and never shows the offline banner on the website or the Mac app.
2. GP — Same structure as QT, but the search hint uses `platform.macos`, so that line is clear. The `.supported` flags still leave open whether they are fixed per build or checked in the browser at runtime. The "works in the app" fallback text is the main hint that the website gets no camera or location.
3. QT — Same as GP, but `modifierKey === '⌘'` doesn't say which platforms give ⌘, for example an iPhone with no keyboard. Only the fallback text tells you what the website gets.
4. LX — You have to work out that unsupported features throw and get caught. The website's result only shows up through `@catch` branches, a `try {}` around haptics, and a note set after a failed Share. The pieces are also split across small components, so it takes more effort to follow, and the Share button always appears.
5. HN — Nothing in the code separates the website from the iPhone. Camera, location and Share are always rendered, and the code gives no hint of what happens where a feature is missing.

B: I trust VR most, because on the website Share, Take photo and the camera simply aren't rendered, so nothing can be a dead button. It is fixed in the code, not something a library has to report correctly. QT and GP come next, but they depend on `.supported` being accurate in the browser. I trust HN least, because Share and Take photo always appear and nothing shows what they do on the website: they could fail silently or throw. LX also always shows Share, but a failed press at least shows "Sharing works in the app.", so the button isn't silent. Like is a partial no-op in every version, since its haptic tap does nothing on the website.

C: Yes, in four ways. All four are inferences from comparing the versions, not facts about the library:

- **Camera and location (1a, 1b):** VR and LX show the same "works in the app" fallbacks deliberately. That makes me more confident the website shows the fallback sentences, not a camera or location prompt.
- **Like (1c, 3):** LX wraps `haptics.tap` in try/catch, which suggests an unsupported call might throw, not silently do nothing. In QT, that would mean pressing Like on the website raises an uncaught error after `likes++` has run. The count still goes up, but there could be a console error.
- **Offline banner (1d):** VR shows it only on the phones, which makes me less sure that `network.supported` is true on the website.
- **Search hint (2f):** GP and VR use `platform.macos` where QT uses `modifierKey`, which suggests ⌘ may be limited to the Mac and an iPhone may show "Ctrl+K". I'm still not sure.

My confidence for question 1 stays at about 2–3 out of 5.
