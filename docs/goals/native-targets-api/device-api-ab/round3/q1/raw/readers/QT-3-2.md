## Ranking
1. VR — It names the platforms outright (`platform.ios || platform.android`, `platform.macos`), so you can read off exactly what a website visitor and an iPhone user each get. The cost is that it hard-codes what each platform can do instead of asking the feature, so it would hide the camera from a Mac app or browser that has one.
2. QT — Every feature is checked with its own `.supported` before it shows, and the fallback text ("works in the app") hints that the website is the unsupported case. You still have to guess what each `.supported` returns on each platform. The search hint uses `modifierKey`, which is self-explanatory.
3. GP — Same as QT, but the search hint uses `platform.macos`. That mixes two ideas, and it is less clear whether a browser on a Mac counts as `macos`, or whether `macos` means only the Mac app.
4. LX — Support is learned by catching errors, so you cannot tell what renders until something throws. You also can't tell whether `camera()` throws during render or later. Share always shows, and on the website you only find out it is unsupported after pressing it and getting the note.
5. HN — There are no checks at all. Nothing in the code tells you what a website visitor gets. Camera, location and Share all render everywhere, and only the library knows what happens.

B: I would trust VR most, with QT and GP close behind. VR shows Share only on iPhone and Android, so the website never gets the button. QT and GP guard Share with `sharesheet.supported`. All three call `haptics.tap` without a check, but Like still counts, so it is never a dead button. I would trust HN least: Share and Take photo always render, and on the website they may do nothing or fail silently. LX is next to last: its Share button does respond on the website, but only with a "Sharing works in the app." note after the press.

C: Yes, a little. Seeing VR and GP make the platform split explicit (the `platform.ios || platform.android` checks, `platform.macos`) made me more confident that in the first version, QT, the website is meant to be the unsupported case for camera and location. So I would firm up 1a and 1b to "you see the 'works in the app' text." It also suggests that `network.supported` and `sharesheet.supported` may be false on the website, so in 1d and 1e the offline banner and Share may simply not appear there. LX's `try` around `haptics.tap` made me less sure that the unguarded call in QT is safe on the website (1c). My answer to 2f, Ctrl+K on iPhone, stays the same: `modifierKey` is unknown there, and GP's `platform.macos` would also give Ctrl+K.
