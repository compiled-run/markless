## Ranking
1. RZ — `cameraApp.open()` plainly says "the phone's own camera app". It sits next to `settings.open()`, `photoLibrary.open()` and `sharesheet.open()`, which follow the same pattern, so every call to a phone screen is `.open()` on a named thing. The on-page camera is the only instance (`cam = camera()`), and it is the one that calls `takePhoto()`.
2. TJ — `systemCamera.open()` clearly means "the OS camera", and the `.open()` pattern is consistent. "system" is a little more abstract than "app", and `systemCamera` sounds like a camera *device* almost as much as a screen.
3. NB — The pattern is consistent, but `cameraScreen` can be read as the on-page preview, the very thing `<base.camera>` shows. That makes it the easiest of the `.open()` names to confuse with `cam`.
4. WK — The verb names (`openSettings`, `openShareSheet`, `choosePhoto`) read well. But the bare `takePhoto()` (phone screen) next to `cam.takePhoto()` (page camera) is the same verb doing two different things. Nothing in the name says that one opens the phone's camera.
5. FD — `camera()` is the page camera and `camera.takePhoto()` is the phone's camera screen, but the two differ only by a pair of parentheses. That is the easiest pair of the five to mix up. It is also inconsistent: `choosePhoto()` doesn't say it opens a screen either.

B: `cameraApp` reads best. "App" says outright that this is the phone's own camera application, a separate screen that leaves the page. `systemCamera` comes close but could mean the hardware. `cameraScreen` could mean any screen showing a camera, including the preview on this page.

C: No answers changed, but one was confirmed and sharpened. In answer 5 I flagged that `cameraScreen` could be read as the on-page preview. Seeing `cameraApp` and `systemCamera` confirmed that it is the weakest `.open()` name for that role. Answers 1–4 stand as I gave them for version NB.
