## Ranking
1. TJ — Every phone-owned screen is an object with `.open()` (`settings`, `systemCamera`, `photoLibrary`, `sharesheet`). `systemCamera` says plainly that it is the phone's own camera, so it can't be confused with the page camera `camera()`/`cam`.
2. RZ — It has the same consistent `.open()` pattern. `cameraApp` reads as "the camera app", but "App" is a weaker signal than "system", and `camera` versus `cameraApp` differ only by a suffix.
3. NB — It has the same `.open()` pattern, but `cameraScreen` is ambiguous: `<base.camera>` is also a camera shown on a screen. It could mean the in-page view or a full-screen camera, not necessarily the phone's own.
4. WK — The verb-style names (`openSettings`, `openShareSheet`, `choosePhoto`) are readable. But the bare `takePhoto()` sits right beside `cam.takePhoto()`, and from the names alone nothing says one takes a photo with the page camera and the other launches the phone's camera.
5. FD — `camera.takePhoto()` versus `cam.takePhoto()` is the worst pair. The same `camera` import is both the factory for the page camera and a namespace that opens the phone's camera, and the two calls differ only in `camera` versus `cam`. That is very easy to mix up and to misread.

B: `systemCamera` reads best. "System" marks the screen as the phone's own, matching what `settings` and `sharesheet` imply, and it can't be read as the page's camera. `cameraApp` comes second: it is clear, but "app" suggests leaving for a separate application, which isn't quite accurate on every platform, and it is close to `camera`. `cameraScreen` is weakest because the page camera is also on screen.

C: Mostly no; the comparison confirmed my answers. In answer 5 I had flagged `camera()` versus `cameraApp` as the pair easiest to confuse. Seen next to FD and WK, RZ's naming is much safer than I rated it, and TJ's `systemCamera` would have removed even that small doubt. My answers 1–4 about RZ stay the same.
