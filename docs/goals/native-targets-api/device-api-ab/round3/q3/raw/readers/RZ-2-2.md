## Ranking
1. TJ — `systemCamera.open()` against `camera()` says plainly that one is the phone's own camera screen and the other is the in-page camera. `settings`, `photoLibrary` and `sharesheet` all follow the same `<system thing>.open()` pattern.
2. RZ — `cameraApp.open()` clearly means the phone's camera app. "App" is slightly less exact than "system", and `cameraApp` sits close to `camera` when you scan the imports.
3. NB — `cameraScreen.open()` follows the `.open()` pattern, but "screen" is vague. It could be read as an in-page camera screen, the same as what `<base.camera>` shows.
4. WK — the bare verbs `openSettings()`, `openShareSheet()` and `choosePhoto()` read fine. But `takePhoto()` next to `cam.takePhoto()` is a trap: the same name means "open the phone camera" in one place and "capture from the in-page camera" in the other, and nothing in the name says a system screen opens.
5. FD — `camera.takePhoto()` against `cam.takePhoto()` is the worst case. The two calls differ only by `camera` versus `cam`, and `camera` is also the factory you call to get the in-page camera. You cannot tell from the code that one of them leaves the page.

B: `systemCamera` reads best. "System" names exactly what makes it different: the phone's own UI, outside the page. It also fits next to `settings`, `photoLibrary` and `sharesheet`. `cameraApp` comes second: it is clear, but "app" may suggest launching a separate app rather than a capture screen that hands back a photo. `cameraScreen` is the most ambiguous of the three.

C: No answers changed in substance. The first version was RZ. Seeing the others backed up my answer 5: the risk of mixing up `camera` and `cameraApp` is real, and the FD and WK versions show how much worse it gets when the names overlap fully (`takePhoto`). It also made my answer 3 point firmer: in every version, only the phone-camera and photo-library calls are used with `?? last`, which is consistent with them being the ones that can come back empty.
