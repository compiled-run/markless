A: PY (`sharesheet`) reads clearer next to `shared`. `share` and `shared` differ by one letter, so at a glance they look like one word in two tenses. A reader could easily think they are related, for example that `shared` is the result of `share`. `sharesheet` names a concrete phone UI, the native share sheet. That sets it apart from `shared`, which is a state helper, and `.open()` then reads naturally as "open the sheet".

B: Yes, `share` could be mistaken for `shared`, in three ways:
- Someone could type or autocomplete the wrong one, or misread it while skimming the imports.
- A reader could assume `share` is the verb behind `shared()`, as in "share this state across components". The two come from different packages, but that is easy to miss.
- `share` also looks like the browser's `navigator.share` and could be taken for a generic function. A reader might expect `share({ title, url })` and not `share.open(...)`. The bare noun-or-verb doesn't say whether it is an action, an object or a permission.

`sharesheet` is unlikely to be confused with `shared`. Its own risks are smaller:
- It's an iOS-flavoured term, so Android or web developers may know the thing as a "share dialog" or "share menu". That's a minor unfamiliarity, not a misreading.
- It could be read as "a sheet of shared things", but that's a stretch.
