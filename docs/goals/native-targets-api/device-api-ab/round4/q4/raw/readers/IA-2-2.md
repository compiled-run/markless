## Ranking
1. IA — It is the only version where the reader can see the website's version directly in the code: `ios.*`, `android.*` and `web.*` branches, all imported from `@markless/ui`. The cost is three copies of everything, and it still doesn't say whether `web.sheet` is drawn by the browser or by the package.
2. NT — `native` clearly says "drawn by the platform, not by the page", and the import shows it comes from `@markless/ui`. What the website gets has to be guessed. I would guess the browser's own built-in elements, such as `<dialog>` and `<video>`, which makes sense but is not shown.
3. SY — `system` says the operating system or browser draws it, and the source is clear. It is a little weaker than `native`. "System" could also mean the app's design system, and the word gives no hint about the website.
4. PF — It says the tag adapts to each platform, but the source is confusing. `platform` is imported from `@markless/device`, so the camera and sheet seem to come from the device package rather than from `@markless/ui`. It also shares an object with the `platform.ios` checks (see C).
5. BC — `base` points the wrong way. Next to `base.button`, it reads as an unstyled building block the author styles, which is the opposite of "the system draws it". Nothing suggests the platforms differ.
6. BT — Bare `<camera>` and `<sheet>` are not imported, so they read as HTML or custom elements, or as `camera` being the `camera()` function from `@markless/device`. Nothing tells you where they come from, who draws them, or what the website gets.

B: `native` says "drawn by the platform, not by the page" best. In app development it already means the platform's own controls, and on the web it maps naturally onto the browser's built-in elements. `system` is a close second, but it collides with "design system". The per-platform names say where each version runs, not who draws it. `platform` says the tag adapts per platform, not who draws it. `base` suggests the opposite. A bare tag says nothing.

C: It can be confused. `platform.ios` is a true/false check and `platform.sheet` is a component, yet both are fields on the same object. A reader might think `platform.sheet` means "the sheet for the current platform" in some special or conditional way. They might also think it is a fact about the device, like `platform.ios`, rather than something on screen. Or they might wonder whether `platform` has a field for every component. It also makes the device package look like the source of the UI components, which blurs the split between `@markless/device` and `@markless/ui`. It also raises a question about what happens if a platform and a component ever share a name.

D: NT. It is one tag with one copy of the heading and buttons, so changing "Choose a filter" means one edit, and `native` states the intent. I would use IA's per-platform branches only where the platforms really need different content.

E: Mostly no, but two answers got sharper:
- Questions 4 and 5: IA's three copies now look like a choice of style rather than something the framework requires. The same sheet fits in one tag in the other versions, so the extra copies are a cost of the per-platform spelling, not a property of the sheet.
- Question 3: seeing `base.camera` and `base.sheet` next to `base.button` made me more confident that `base` means a cross-platform building block the author styles. That supports my reading that the drawer is styled by the author.

My answers to questions 2 and 6, and my confidence in them, are unchanged. Every version keeps `@if (platform.ios)` for the note, so whether `platform` means the build or the device's operating system is still unresolved.
