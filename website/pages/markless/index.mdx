The idea underneath is small. When you create a variable, create it with `state`. Whatever
reads it stays current, and the screen is one of the things reading it.

Hover a line if you don't believe it. Then click the heart:

<HeroCard />

You just changed a variable. The number followed, because `state` told the page to follow
`likes`. That is the whole trick.

## Nothing hidden

Too simple, right? Surely something is re-running your code on every click. Good instinct,
wrong framework. This heart keeps a log of every time your function runs:

<RunOnceDemo />

Your function ran once. Every click since is just `likes++`. Your AI can't hallucinate what
doesn't exist. There's nothing hidden under the page: one run, then plain updates. And when
code can't compile safely, the build fails and says why, which your AI can easily fix.

## One language

A component is a TypeScript function in a `.tsrx` file. There is no template language. Add the
router and a file under `pages/` is a URL, with no routing config either. That is the whole
learning curve, for you and for your agent.

## Not married to the web

TSRX, the language underneath, also compiles to React, Solid and Vue. Nothing about a compiled
interface insists on a browser either. And the speed people notice is a side effect: every update
is written in advance. [How it works](/markless/how-it-works) shows the machinery.
